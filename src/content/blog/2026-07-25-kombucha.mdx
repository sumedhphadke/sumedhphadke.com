*Your keep-forever kombucha guide*

You've got a living thing now. Treat it like a low-maintenance pet, and it'll feed you for months. *Everything you need to keep your booch alive is on this card. Read it once, then trust it.*

**👐 = steps where your hands touch the culture or the liquid. Clean, soap-free hands only.**

## What You'll Need

*for one 1-litre jar — your at-home batch*

* **Water**: 1 litre. Filtered or boiled-and-cooled. Chlorine from straight tap water stresses the culture; boiling for 3- 5 min will remove it.  
* **Black Tea**: 8g loose leaf, or 3 tea bags. Plain black or green only. Nothing flavoured; the oils in blends like Earl Grey harm the scoby. Assam Tea CTC is forgiving and beginner-friendly.  
* **Sugar**: 60g (about 4 level tbsp), plain white. This is food for the culture, not for you. Most of it gets eaten during the ferment.  
  *Better to go over than under with Tea and Sugar*  
* **Starter liquid**: 200ml of mature kombucha from a prior batch (or the liquid your scoby came home in). This is your acidity and your mould insurance. Don't skip it.  
* **SCOBY**: 1. Also called the mother or pellicle.  
* **Kit**: a 1L **wide-mouth glass jar**, **a cloth cover** + **rubber band**, and a plastic or wooden spoon. No metal: kombucha is acidic and doesn't like prolonged metal contact. A quick touch is fine; a steel lid sitting on it for days is not.

**The ratio to remember:  80% sweet tea, 20% starter.**  For a 1L jar, that's ~800ml tea + ~200ml starter. Memorise this one thing, and you never need the card again.

## The Sweet Tea

*your base*

Boil the water for 3 minutes (longer if you think the water is chlorinated), take it off the heat, and steep the tea for 10-15 minutes. Filter the tea out, then stir the sugar in until it's fully dissolved. Now the slow part: **cool it completely to room temperature.** Not warm, not lukewarm, properly cool. Warm tea kills the scoby, and there's no undo. If you're impatient, sit the covered pot in a sink of cold water to speed it up.

*(Today, we made this for you, so in the session you start straight at 1F below.)*

### 1F — First Fermentation

1. Make sure the jar is dry. If it's been sitting where dust could reach inside, rinse it one last time.  
2. **👐** Wash your hands with soap, then rinse until no soap is left on them. Any soap residue will kill the scoby.  
3. Pour in the tea.  
4. Cut open the scoby packet close to the jar, not over the top of it.  
5. **👐** Pour in the liquid from the packet first. That's your starter.  
6. **👐** Lift the scoby out and place it in. Minimal contact: lift it from the packet and set it gently into the tea. It might float, it might sink. Either is fine.  
7. **👐** If the tea or the scoby sloshes around the inner walls of the jar, wipe it with a fresh kitchen towel, wipe downwards close to the tea level, but do not touch the tea. Make sure the rest of the jar is dry; sweet tea above the SCOBY level could grow mould.  
8. Cover the mouth with the cloth and rubber band, **not** the lid. It needs to breathe; the cloth still keeps dust and flies out.  
9. Write today's date on the jar or a strip of tape. Future-you will want to know when this began.  
10. Find it a home: warm-ish, out of direct sun, undisturbed. Away from the bin, houseplants, and other ferments. Not the fridge; cold stops everything.  
11. Now leave it alone. Genuinely the hardest step. No stirring, no peeking under the cloth. Roughly 7–10 days in Mumbai's warmth.  
12. **👐** Start tasting around day 5 or 6 with a clean straw or spoon, fresh each time, no double-dipping.  
    Too sweet = give it more days.  
    Sharp and vinegary = you left it a little long (still usable).  
    **Tart-with-a-hint-of-sweet is your ready.**

## 2F — Second Fermentation

*flavour & fizz*

### What You'll Need

*for one 1-litre bottle — your flavoured batch*

* **Classic Kombucha**: Sour-tasting yet fruity kombucha from 1F.  
* **Flavours**: Any flavours of your choice. Sweet sugary fruits make fizzy Kombucha. The culture loves ginger, with skin. Ripe mango, berries, apple, beet, peach make for great flavours. Mix and match, make your own.   
  Avoid raw honey, peppers, unprocessed sugar, and mushy but sweet fruits like bananas and melons for now. Cinnamon and other spices are better added late in the process.  
  *No commercial juices, preservatives whatsoever*.   
* **Sugar** (Optional): Sugar might help add some fizz if you like your kombucha fizzy. Decide after tasting your flavour and your liking.  
* **A ceramic, plastic or glass bowl or plate**: To keep your SCOBY while you pour out your Kombucha.  
* **Muslin cloth**: Unbleached muslin cloth for filtering SCOBY particles.  
* **Funnel**: To pour your kombucha into bottles  
* **Bottles:** Glass bottles. Flip-top bottle caps work best; a tight seal is important for a good fizz.

### Steps:

13. Add your flavouring to the bottle: fruit, juice, ginger. A rough guide: 1 tbsp fruit per 100ml. You can strain it through a mesh, but the pulp adds flavour. You can strain it out later.  
14. **👐** Carefully remove the scoby and place it into a glass plate or a bowl; pour in some tea to keep it moist.  
15. Place a funnel lined with muslin cloth over the bottle.  
16. Carefully pour in the kombucha from the jar. Leave at least 1 - 2 cm empty space on a 500ml bottle, 2-3 cm on a 1 L bottle. Be sure to save enough liquid for your next batch, or a SCOBY Hotel. Yup, it keeps going.  
17. **Seal it tight. This is the fizz step**; a firm seal traps the CO2. A loose cap gives you flat booch every time.  
18. Leave it sealed and undisturbed at room temp for 2-4 days. If you're nervous about pressure, "burp" it once a day (open briefly, reseal) — but every burp costs you fizz, so a firm bottle left alone is best. Better to burp it than have the bottle explode.  
19. Move it to the fridge to stop the ferment. Open your first one slowly, over a sink, maybe.

### If Something Looks Off

**The good signs:** wet, glossy, web-like patches forming across the top, and brown stringy bits drifting below. Both normal, both healthy. A tart, vinegary smell is exactly right.

**The one bad sign:** dry, fuzzy, raised spots sitting on top — green, black, blue or white, fuzzy like bread mould. If you see that, bin the whole thing, SCOBY and liquid both, and don't try to rescue it.

**Unsure?** Smell it; tart and sour is happy, musty or rotten is not — or photograph it and message me.

### Need a break?

The SCOBY can be a bit demanding at times; you might just need a break from it. It will stay happily for a couple of weeks in a SCOBY Hotel — an acidic tea mixture. Leave the SCOBY in 200-400ml of kombucha (depending on the size).

If you need a longer break, put this in the fridge, sealed airtight. The SCOBY can stay dormant for about a year or so, waiting for you —  it understands.

You can pull it out of the fridge, let it warm up (hug it even), and all will be forgiven. The SCOBY will pick it up from where you left off.
